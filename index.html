CURATORIAL OVERVIEW

About the Exhibit

A collaborative exhibition by the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (CSMVS), Mumbai, and the British Museum.

Indian textiles were celebrated for their craftsmanship, intricate patterns, materials and techniques. But behind every finished textile was an extensive process involving skilled artisans, specialised tools, raw materials and trade networks.

This exhibition explores two interconnected stages of India's textile heritage: the art of textile manufacturing and the trade that carried textiles and spices across regions and continents.

ROOM 01

The Art of Making

Before a textile became a treasured commodity, it began with the hands of an artisan. From preparing fibres and spinning yarn to weaving, dyeing and printing, every stage required specialised knowledge and skill. This room explores the traditional techniques and tools that shaped India's rich textile-making heritage.

ROOM 02

The Routes of Trade

The story of Indian textiles did not end at the loom. Across centuries, Indian fabrics and spices travelled through extensive networks of merchants, ports and markets. This room explores the commercial journey of these treasured commodities and India's place in historic global trade.
